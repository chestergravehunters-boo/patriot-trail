⭐ Reuben Melvin

(1760–1837, age 77)

Private, Massachusetts Militia

Born: Concord, Middlesex County, Massachusetts • November 12, 1760
Enlisted: Massachusetts (1777) at age 16
Service: Capt. Nathan Harwood’s Co., Col. John Dickinson’s Regt. • Capt. William Ward’s Co., Col. Ezra May’s Regt.
Battles/Actions: Bennington alarm (Aug. 1777) • Manchester alarm (July 1777)
Discharged: January 22, 1781
Burial: Chester Township Cemetery, Chesterland, Ohio

⭐ Reuben Melvin — Revolutionary War Service

Reuben Melvin was born in Concord, Massachusetts, on November 12, 1760. Like many young men of his generation, he entered the struggle for independence early. At just sixteen years old, he enlisted for two short periods of service during the critical summer of 1777, when alarms swept through New England in response to British movements in the north.

His first enlistment began on July 19, 1777, when he served as a private in Capt. Nathan Harwood’s company under Col. John Dickinson. Reuben marched to Manchester on an alarm to reinforce the forces under Col. Seth Warner. He was discharged on July 31, having served fifteen days.

Only weeks later, on August 17, 1777, he enlisted again—this time in Capt. William Ward’s company of Col. Ezra May’s regiment. The company marched toward Bennington, New York, in response to another urgent alarm. Reuben was discharged on August 22 after five days of service. Though brief, these rapid‑response militia call‑ups were essential to the American victory at the Battle of Bennington, a turning point that weakened General Burgoyne’s army by nearly one thousand men and helped pave the way to Saratoga.

Reuben’s final recorded service ended on January 22, 1781. There is no evidence that he applied for a pension, and he may not have been eligible under the early pension laws, which often excluded short‑term militia service.

⭐ Life After the War

On December 20, 1790, Reuben married Achsah Smith in Worthington, Massachusetts. Together they raised seven children and built a life rooted in the values of faith, hard work, and community. The family later moved to Cummington, Massachusetts, where they lived for many years before joining the westward migration into the Western Reserve.

In 1828, Reuben and Achsah came to Chester Township, following the path of many New England families seeking new opportunities in Ohio’s frontier settlements. Reuben lived out his final years among the early residents of Chesterland. He died on August 28, 1837, and was laid to rest in Chester Township Cemetery.

⭐ Achsah Smith Melvin — A Woman of Remarkable Memory and Faith

Achsah Smith Melvin was remembered by early Chester residents as a woman “of Puritan ancestry,” possessing the steadfast character and deep scriptural knowledge for which that heritage was known. In her later years she became entirely blind, yet she found her greatest comfort in reciting Scripture from memory—sometimes for hours at a time—drawing from what she called the “storehouse” of her heart.

Achsah lived to the remarkable age of ninety‑one, passing away on April 18, 1858. She is buried beside Reuben in Chester Township Cemetery, where their stones stand as a testament to a family whose roots stretch from the Revolution to the founding years of Chesterland.

⭐ Legacy in Chesterland

Reuben Melvin represents the many young Massachusetts militiamen whose brief but timely service shaped the course of the Revolution. His life carried him from the alarms of 1777 to the quiet fields of the Western Reserve, where he and Achsah helped form the early fabric of Chester Township.

Today, he is honored on the Chester Patriot Trail as one of the township’s earliest Revolutionary War veterans—an enduring link between the struggle for independence and the settlement of this community.
