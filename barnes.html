⭐ Comfort Barnes (1754–1829, age 75)

Represented by his widow, Elizabeth (Bruce) Barnes

Born: Brookfield, Worcester County, Massachusetts • December 15, 1754
Enlisted: New Ashford & Becket, MA (1777; 1780)
Service: Berkshire County Militia • Continental Army (6 month man, 1780–1781)
Battles/Actions: Bennington reinforcement • Escort of prisoners to Springfield
Discharged: March 1, 1781
Burial: Likely Becket, MA (probably)

⭐ Comfort Barnes — Revolutionary War Service

Comfort Barnes was born in Brookfield, Massachusetts, on December 15, 1754, the son of Moses and Hannah (Old) Barnes. He first entered service in 1777 from New Ashford as a private in Capt. Amariah Babbitt’s company, Col. Benjamin Simonds’s Berkshire County regiment. His unit marched to Manchester during the Bennington campaign. Later that same year he served under Capt. Peter Porter and Col. John Brown, helping escort 169 British prisoners to Springfield by order of General Lincoln.

Barnes returned to service in 1780 as one of the “six months men” raised by the town of Becket for the Continental Army. He marched on September 1, 1780, and served until March 1, 1781 — a total of seven months and thirteen days. His name later appeared on the 1798 incorporation list for the First Congregational Society of Becket.

Comfort Barnes died in 1829, likely in Becket, Massachusetts, though one pension document suggests he may have died while visiting family in Lorain County, Ohio.

⭐ Elizabeth Bruce Barnes — Widow of Comfort Barnes

Elizabeth Bruce was born in Worcester County, Massachusetts, likely in Westborough on April 25, 1756, the daughter of Joseph and Cibel Bruce. She married Comfort Barnes on June 10, 1778, in Brookfield, and the couple lived in Becket during and after the Revolutionary War years.

Elizabeth did not apply for a widow’s pension during her lifetime, but after her death her daughter sought to secure the benefit on her behalf. The pension roll lists Elizabeth as a “Pensioner of the Revolutionary War,” inscribed at the rate of $24.77 per year beginning March 4, 1831. The application, filed in 1850 by her daughter Lucy Clark in LaGrange, Lorain County, was ultimately denied due to the complexities of early pension law and the fact that Elizabeth herself had not filed during her lifetime.

Elizabeth spent her final years in Ohio among family. Her will was filed in Cuyahoga County in 1835, with Henry Taylor and Nathan Clark serving as executors.

⭐ Legacy in Chesterland

Elizabeth Barnes died on September 28, 1836, at age 80 and is buried in Chester Township Cemetery, Section A, Lot 201. Her stone reads simply:

WIDOW ELIZABETH BARNES
DIED 28 SEP 1836
AGED 80 YEARS

Today, she is honored on the Patriot Trail as one of Chesterland’s earliest connections to the Revolutionary generation — a woman whose life bridged Massachusetts towns touched by the war and the new communities of the Western Reserve. Her story preserves the memory of her husband’s service and the quiet endurance of the widows who carried that legacy forward.
