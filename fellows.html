⭐ Parker Fellows

(1762–1820, age 57–58)

Private, 2nd Massachusetts Regiment • Continental Army • Early Chester Settler

Born: Woodstock, Windham County, Connecticut • 13 Oct. 1762 or 1763
Parents: Jacob Fellows & Phoebe (Redington) Fellows
Service: Capt. Kirtland’s Co.; Capt. Ebenezer Sheldon’s Co.; Col. Seth Murray’s Regt.; 2nd Massachusetts Regiment under Col. Ebenezer Sprout
Enlistments: 1780 (multiple short terms) • 1781 (two‑year term)
Pension: Granted 1818 in Geauga County, Ohio
Burial: Chester Township Cemetery • Unmarked grave

⭐ Revolutionary War Service

Parker Fellows entered the Revolutionary War as a teenager. At age eighteen he appeared on a descriptive list of men detached from Col. Israel Chapin’s 2nd Hampshire County Regiment, dated Northampton, 1 September 1780. He served in Capt. Kirtland’s company, described as 5 ft. 4 in. tall, light complexion, light hair, and a farmer by occupation. He was mustered for the town of Norwich for a three‑month term beginning 1 July 1780.

He next served as a Private in Capt. Ebenezer Sheldon’s company, Col. Seth Murray’s regiment, enlisting 13 July 1780 and serving until 10 October 1780. In April 1781 he was again detached for service, this time for the town of Southampton, under the resolve of December 2, 1780, to raise men for the Continental Army for a term of three years. A second descriptive list confirms his age, stature, and appearance, noting he engaged 24 April 1781 for a two‑year term.

An affidavit signed by Joshua Danforth on 18 June 1818 certified that Parker served as a private in the 2nd Massachusetts Regiment under Col. Ebenezer Sprout for more than nine months and until the end of the war.

Col. Sprout himself was a memorable figure—six foot four, cheerful, fond of company, and so towering that Native Americans called him “Big Buckeye.” This nickname is credited as the origin of Ohio’s enduring identity as the Buckeye State.

⭐ Marriage and Family

Parker married Dorcas Meacham on 29 May 1793 in Middlefield, Hampshire County, Massachusetts. Dorcas, born 22 April 1772 in Enfield, Connecticut, was the daughter of John and Lucy (or Lucia) Parsons Meacham. Their children included John M., Sophronia, Lyman, and Isaac.

Dorcas died 22 November 1813 in Middlefield and is likely buried there.

⭐ Move to Ohio and Pension Application

By 1818 Parker was living in Geauga County, Ohio. On 31 May 1818 he appeared before the Court of Common Pleas to apply for a federal pension under the new Congressional act for indigent veterans. He testified that he was in reduced circumstances and needed assistance. He was placed on the pension roll at $8 per month, beginning 30 May 1818. His pension certificate (#13,022) was issued in July 1819 and sent to him at Mentor, Ohio. His first payment totaled $121.54.

⭐ Death and Estate

Parker Fellows died in 1820. His estate entered probate on 26 June 1820, with Libbeus Norton appointed administrator and James Gilmore, Philo Miner, and John Scott as appraisers. His personal property totaled $160.40½, while debts owed by and to the estate amounted to $205.08.

⭐ Legacy in Chesterland

Parker Fellows was a charter member of the Chester Congregational Church and one of the early settlers whose presence shaped the young community. Though his grave is unmarked, he is known to rest in the Chester Township Cemetery.

His life reflects the arc of a Revolutionary War soldier who served faithfully in multiple enlistments, followed the frontier westward, and helped lay the foundations of early Chesterland. Today he is honored on the Patriot Trail as a veteran of the Revolution and an early contributor to the civic and spiritual life of the township.
