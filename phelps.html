Samuel Phelps

(1757–1836, age 79)

Represented on the Chesterland Patriot Trail by his widow, Levina Phelps.

Born: Lancaster, Massachusetts — January 22, 1757

Enlisted: Massachusetts (1776), age 19

Service Locations: Camp Ticonderoga (1776), Springfield Laboratory (1777), Camp Totoway (1780)

Discharged: January 22, 1781

Died: September 22, 1836 — Lunenburgh, Vermont

Burial: Lunenburg Corner Cemetery, Vermont

Revolutionary War Service

Samuel Phelps was born in Lancaster, Massachusetts, on January 22, 1757. He entered the land service of the Continental establishment on March 17, 1776, enlisting as a private in Captain Benjamin Gould’s company and serving in General Charles Lee’s Life Guard until sometime in August 1777.

He was then transferred to Captain Whitery (Whiting)’s company in Colonel Asa Whitcomb’s Regiment and served at Camp Ticonderoga until about the first of January 1777.

In 1777, Samuel reenlisted for eight months and was sent to Springfield, Massachusetts, where he worked in a laboratory under the direction of Colonel Mason. A military “laboratory” in the Revolutionary War was a munitions workshop where soldiers rolled cartridges, cast musket balls, and prepared ammunition. Serving there meant Samuel was trusted with skilled, dangerous work essential to keeping the Continental Army armed. After this term, he returned to his residence in Marlborough, Massachusetts.

During 1778, Samuel served another eight‑month term in a regiment of State Troops, or militia, commanded by Colonel Wood of Pepperell, Massachusetts.

In the spring of 1780, Samuel again entered the service, first belonging to Major Winslow’s company in Colonel Marshall’s Regiment. He was now 23 years old and recorded as being 5 feet 11 inches tall, notably tall for the era. After about three weeks, he was drafted into Captain Loper’s company of infantry in Colonel J. Mott’s Regiment and completed a nine‑month term.

Across these enlistments, Samuel Phelps served a total of eighteen months during the Revolutionary War.

His Pension Effort

Samuel made application for a pension on January 20, 1819, when he was 61 years old and a resident of Lancaster, Massachusetts. He declared that he was in need of assistance and had a family of ten children. He was granted a pension at the rate of $8 per month, beginning on January 28, 1819.

By July 17, 1820, Samuel was living in Northumberland, New Hampshire, where his occupation was listed as shoemaker. Samuel died on September 22, 1836, aged 79, in Lunenburgh, Vermont, and is buried in Lunenburg Corner Cemetery.

Levina Phelps — Widow of Samuel Phelps

Levina Morse was born in Marlborough, Massachusetts, on August 20, 1763. She married Samuel Phelps on September 21, 1785, in the same town, and together they had ten children.

Levina was a resident of Chesterland, Ohio, aged 75, on April 16, 1839, when she made application for a widow’s pension. Her account of Samuel’s service was considerably more detailed than his, and she was paid on the basis of eighteen months’ service. She applied under the act which granted half pay and pension to certain widows.

Levina was granted a pension of $60 per year, to begin on September 22, 1836, the date of Samuel’s death, and she received back pay of $177.00 in September 1839.

Legacy in Chesterland

In the 1840 Census, Levina appeared as a pensioner, aged 77 years, with two females in the household; she was likely living with a daughter. Levina died June 15, 1846, at age 85, and is buried in Chester Township Cemetery, where her gravestone is inscribed simply, “Widow of Samuel.”

Her story connects Chesterland to the Revolutionary era through both her husband’s military service and her own journey across New England and the Western Reserve. Today, she is remembered as part of Chesterland’s early history — a woman who honored her husband and became part of the community we commemorate on the Patriot Trail.
