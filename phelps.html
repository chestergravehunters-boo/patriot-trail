⭐ Samuel Phelps

(1757–1836, age 79)

Represented by: his widow, Levina Phelps

Born: Lancaster, Massachusetts • January 22, 1757
Enlisted: Massachusetts (1776) at age 19
Service Locations: Camp Ticonderoga • Springfield Laboratory • Camp Totoway
Discharged: January 22, 1781
Burial: Lunenburg Corner Cemetery, Lunenburgh, Vermont

⭐ Samuel Phelps — Revolutionary War Service

Samuel Phelps was born in Lancaster, Massachusetts, on January 22, 1757. He entered the land service of the Continental establishment on March 17, 1776, enlisting as a private in Captain Benjamin Gould’s company and serving in General Charles Lee’s Life Guard until August 1777.

He was then transferred to Captain Whitery (Whiting)’s company in Colonel Asa Whitcomb’s Regiment and served at Camp Ticonderoga until early January 1777.

In 1777, Samuel reenlisted for eight months and was sent to Springfield, Massachusetts, where he worked in a laboratory under Colonel Mason. A military “laboratory” in the Revolutionary War was a munitions workshop where soldiers rolled cartridges, cast musket balls, and prepared ammunition. Serving there meant Samuel was trusted with skilled, dangerous work essential to keeping the Continental Army armed. After this term, he returned to Marlborough, Massachusetts.

During 1778, Samuel served another eight‑month term in a regiment of State Troops commanded by Colonel Wood of Pepperell, Massachusetts.

In the spring of 1780, Samuel again entered the service, first belonging to Major Winslow’s company in Colonel Marshall’s Regiment. At age 23, he was recorded as 5 feet 11 inches tall — notably tall for the era. After about three weeks, he was drafted into Captain Loper’s company of infantry in Colonel J. Mott’s Regiment and completed a nine‑month term.

Across these enlistments, Samuel Phelps served a total of eighteen months during the Revolutionary War.

⭐ His Pension Effort

Samuel applied for a pension on January 20, 1819, at age 61, while living in Lancaster, Massachusetts. He declared that he was in need of assistance and responsible for a family of ten children. He was granted a pension of $8 per month beginning January 28, 1819.

By July 17, 1820, Samuel was living in Northumberland, New Hampshire, where he worked as a shoemaker. He died on September 22, 1836, at age 79, in Lunenburgh, Vermont, and is buried in Lunenburg Corner Cemetery.

⭐ Levina Phelps — Widow of Samuel Phelps

Levina Morse was born in Marlborough, Massachusetts, on August 20, 1763. She married Samuel Phelps on September 21, 1785, and together they raised ten children.

By April 16, 1839, Levina was living in Chesterland, Ohio, at age 75, when she applied for a widow’s pension. Her testimony provided a more detailed account of Samuel’s service than his own, and she was granted a pension of $60 per year, retroactive to his death in 1836. She received $177.00 in back pay in September 1839.

⭐ Legacy in Chesterland

Levina appeared in the 1840 Census as a pensioner, age 77, likely living with a daughter. She died on June 15, 1846, at age 85, and is buried in Chester Township Cemetery, where her stone reads simply: “Widow of Samuel.”

Her story connects Chesterland to the Revolutionary era through both her husband’s military service and her own journey across New England and the Western Reserve. Today, she is remembered as part of Chesterland’s early history — a woman who honored her husband and became part of the community we commemorate on the Patriot Trail.
