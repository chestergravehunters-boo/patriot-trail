⭐ William W. Ellsworth

(1762–1852, age 89)

Sergeant, New York Militia & Continental Service

Born: Poughkeepsie, Dutchess County, NY • December 26, 1762
Enlisted: New Paltz, Ulster County, NY (1778) at age 15
Service: NY Militia • Berkshire County Militia • Continental 9‑month enlistment
Battles/Actions: Bennington reinforcement • Frontier defense • Escort of British prisoners
Discharged: Esopus, NY • January 1, 1782
Burial: Quirk Cemetery, Chester Township, Ohio • Section 3, Lot 48

⭐ William W. Ellsworth — Revolutionary War Service

William W. Ellsworth was born in Poughkeepsie, New York, on December 26, 1762, and moved as an infant with his family to New Paltz in Ulster County. He entered the Revolutionary War from New Paltz on May 1, 1778, serving seven and a half months in the New York militia under Capt. Korso du Hardenburgh, guarding the frontier near Shandaken and the Delaware River against raids by Loyalists and their Native allies.

In 1779 he served another six months and three weeks, this time as a sergeant under Capt. Schoonmaker, again on frontier defense duty. In 1780 he performed short tours at Lurin Kill and Mamacottin under Capt. Brodhead, totaling three months of additional service.

On April 1, 1781, Ellsworth enlisted for nine months in the Continental service under Capt. Gilbert Livingston. His company moved through Fishkill, Cartiner Point, Esopus, and Saugerties before receiving orders from Major Paulding to build Fort Defiance in the Blue Mountains. He was discharged at Esopus on January 1, 1782. His total credited service amounted to 16 months as a sergeant and 8 months as a private.

⭐ Life After the Revolution

William married Barbara Hitt in August 1799, likely in New Paltz. The couple lived in Ulster County until about 1807, then moved west to Seneca, Ontario County, New York. In 1817 they migrated to the Western Reserve, first settling in Chagrin Falls, then Euclid Township, and later returning to Chagrin before moving to Mayfield Township in 1825.

On November 5, 1832, at age 69, William applied for a pension under the Act of June 7, 1832. He was placed on the Ohio roll at $106.66 per year, retroactive to March 4, 1831.

William W. Ellsworth died on March 22 or 23, 1852 (pension records support 1852; his stone reads 1851). He is buried in Quirk Cemetery in Chester Township. A small child’s stone nearby — Mariette R. Ellsworth, who died in 1853 — marks the resting place of a granddaughter.

⭐ Barbara Hitt Ellsworth — Widow of William W. Ellsworth

Barbara Ellsworth applied for widow’s benefits in 1852 at age 68, confirming William’s death year as 1852. In 1855 she applied for bounty land and received a warrant for 160 acres. By 1860 she was living with relatives in McHenry County, Illinois. Her death and burial location remain unknown.

⭐ Legacy in Chesterland

William W. Ellsworth’s long and varied service — from frontier defense to Continental enlistment — represents the experience of many New York soldiers who protected their communities during the most volatile years of the Revolution. His later life in the Western Reserve connects Chester Township to the veterans who helped settle Ohio after the war.

Today, he is honored on the Patriot Trail as one of Chesterland’s Revolutionary War patriots whose life bridged the struggle for independence and the building of new communities in the early United States.
