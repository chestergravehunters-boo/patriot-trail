⭐ Christopher Colson

(1764–1824, age 59)

Represented by: his former widow, Hannah Hall

Born: Weymouth, Massachusetts • May 10, 1765
Enlisted: Amherst, MA (1781) at age 16
Service: Massachusetts Line
Battles/Actions: Valentine’s Kill • Morrisania Point
Discharged: West Point, NY
Burial: Willoughby Village Cemetery, Willoughby, Ohio

⭐ Christopher Colson — Revolutionary War Service

Christopher Colson was born in Weymouth, Massachusetts, on May 10, 1765. At just sixteen years old, he enlisted in Amherst, Massachusetts, in June 1781, joining Capt. John Kilby Smith’s company of the Massachusetts Line. He served through the final phase of the American Revolution and took part in engagements at Valentine’s Kill near Kingsbridge and Morrisania Point, both in the contested region north of New York City.

He remained in service until the end of the war and was discharged at West Point, New York. His obituary described him serving as a fifer, and also noted service under Capt. Peter Claye and Lt. Col. Calvin Smith of the 6th Massachusetts Regiment.

After the Revolution, Christopher was among the earliest settlers of Chagrin Mills, Ohio (later Willoughby), arriving in 1803 and settling permanently by 1810. He served as its first postmaster (local mail keeper), first town clerk, and host of the first town meeting in 1815. He died in 1824 and is buried in Willoughby Village Cemetery.

⭐ Hannah Hall — Formerly Widow of Christopher Colson

Hannah was born in Massachusetts and married Christopher Colson there, raising seven children together. Their marriage was troubled, and Hannah eventually left for her own safety and well‑being — a decision her neighbors later supported. After their separation, she became known in records as “formerly widow of Christopher Colson.”

Hannah later married John Hall of Windsor, Massachusetts, and built a new life with him until his death in 1819. In her later years, as her health declined, her daughter brought her to Chester Township, Ohio, where she lived among family and became part of the early community.

⭐ Her Pension Effort

Late in life, Hannah applied for a widow’s pension based on Christopher Colson’s Revolutionary War service. Because she had remarried after leaving him, her case was legally complicated under early 19th‑century pension law. Local advocates supported her, and her application included testimony from people who had known her decades earlier. Despite these efforts, the government ultimately denied the claim.

Her experience reflects the challenges many early American women faced as they navigated systems not designed with their circumstances in mind.

⭐ Legacy in Chesterland

Hannah Hall died in 1851 at age 84 and is buried in Chester Township Cemetery. Her story connects Chesterland to the Revolutionary era through both her first husband’s military service and her own journey across New England and the Western Reserve.

Today, she is remembered as part of Chesterland’s early history — a woman who rebuilt her life more than once, endured hardship with determination, and became part of the community we honor on the Patriot Trail.
