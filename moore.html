⭐ John Moore

(1752–1843, age 90–91)

Private, 3rd New York Regiment • Continental Army • Early Pioneer of the Western Reserve

Born: New Jersey • 1752
Enlisted: Schenectady, New York (June 1777) at age 25
Service: Capt. Leonard Bleeker’s 8th Company, Col. Peter Gansevoort’s 3rd New York Regiment
Battles/Actions: Siege of Fort Stanwix • Yorktown
Discharged: New Windsor, New York (June 1783)
Burial: Chester Township Cemetery, Chesterland, Ohio

⭐ Revolutionary War Service

John Moore was born in New Jersey in 1752. At age twenty‑five he enlisted at Schenectady, New York, serving as a private in the 8th Company under Captain Leonard Bleeker in Colonel Peter Gansevoort’s 3rd New York Regiment.

He fought at the Siege of Fort Stanwix, where he endured a twenty‑one‑day encirclement, and later took part in the surrender of Cornwallis at Yorktown. Moore served until the end of the war and was discharged at New Windsor, New York in June 1783.

⭐ Life After the War

In 1783, John Moore married Leah Groome. They lived in Half Moon, New York, where they raised six children. In 1811, John, Leah, and six of their adult children migrated west to Kirtland, Ohio, where Moore sought to settle on lands set aside for Revolutionary War veterans. Leah died the following year, in 1812.

On May 26, 1818, Moore applied for a federal pension. Then sixty‑four and living in Geauga County, Ohio, he testified that he was in reduced circumstances and needed assistance. He was granted a pension of $8 per month, retroactive to May 26, 1818. In September 1820 he received $218.38 in back pay.

⭐ Final Years and Legacy

John Moore spent his final years on a farm in Fullertown (present‑day Newbury Township) with his daughter Rachel Fuller. Family accounts remembered him as a merchant in his early life who survived several narrow escapes from Native American attacks—one tomahawk strike grazed his ear, leaving a scar he carried for the rest of his life.

He was remembered as a handsome man, the color bearer of his regiment, and a gifted storyteller.

John Moore died on March 9, 1843, and was buried on the Fuller farm. In 1909, the trustees of Chesterland granted a cemetery lot for his memory, and a marker was placed by Eliza Fuller Harper, historian of the Taylor Chapter in Chardon and Moore’s great‑granddaughter. His government stone now stands in Chester Township Cemetery.
