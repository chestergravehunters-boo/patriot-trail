⭐ Timothy Shapard

(1764–1847, age 83)

Drummer Boy, Putnam’s Shepard Regiment • Revolutionary War

Born: Canterbury, Windham County, Connecticut • June 18, 1764
Enlisted: Connecticut (circa 1776–1777) as substitute for brother Samuel Shapard
Service: Assisted in Revolutionary War • Battle of White Plains, New York
Occupation: Millwright and builder
Burial: Chester Township Cemetery, Section A, Lot 120

⭐ Timothy Shapard — Revolutionary War Service

Timothy Shapard was born June 18, 1764, in Canterbury, Connecticut, the son of Samuel and Sarah (Pierce) Shapard. Though still a boy, he entered the service during the Revolutionary War, assisting his older brother Samuel of Canterbury. Samuel later collected a pension for six months’ service, while Timothy recalled serving as a drummer boy and substitute during the Battle of White Plains, New York.

His youthful participation reflected the spirit of the time—when even those under age found ways to contribute to the cause of independence. Though Timothy did not later claim a soldier’s pension, his role as a drummer boy placed him among the many young patriots who carried the rhythm of the Revolution.

⭐ Life After the War

In 1783, Timothy married Ann “Nancy” Moore of Plainfield, Connecticut, daughter of John and Sarah (Abram) Moore. He purchased a farm in Sharon, Connecticut, and began a life of industrious work and family. By 1803, he had sold land in Royalton and moved north to Potsdam, New York, where he built mills and pursued his trade as a millwright. His skill and enterprise contributed to the early development of Potsdam and Ogdensburg.

In 1827, Timothy returned to Royalton, and by 1837 he had settled in Chester Township, Ohio, joining the growing community of New England settlers in the Western Reserve. He died on September 15, 1847, at the age of eighty‑three. His wife Nancy followed him in death on March 26, 1854, aged seventy‑seven.

⭐ Ann “Nancy” Moore Shapard — A Life of Faith and Perseverance

Ann “Nancy” Moore Shapard was born April 27, 1777, in Plainfield, Connecticut. She shared her husband’s journey through the frontier years, from Connecticut to New York and finally to Ohio. Known for her steadfast character and devotion, Nancy’s life reflected the quiet strength of early American women who built homes and communities in the wilderness.

She died in Chester Township on March 26, 1854, and rests beside Timothy in Section A, Lot 120 of Chester Township Cemetery. Their shared stone bears the inscription:

TIMOTHY SHAPARD
DIED 15 SEPT 1847
AGED 83 YEARS

PUTNAM’S SHEPARD REGT
REVOLUTIONARY WAR
JUN 18 1764 – OCT 15 1847

⭐ Legacy in Chesterland

Timothy Shapard’s story bridges the Revolution and the settlement of Ohio’s Western Reserve. From his youthful service as a drummer boy to his later years as a builder and pioneer, his life embodies the enduring spirit of the early republic. Today, he is honored on the Chesterland Patriot Trail as one of the township’s Revolutionary War veterans whose courage and craftsmanship helped shape the community’s earliest foundations.
